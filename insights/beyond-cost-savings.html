Global Talent & Outsourcing

Beyond Cost Savings: How Outsourcing Helps Businesses Close Skills Gaps and Access Experienced Global Talent

Look beyond employment costs to the experience, skills and working arrangements that can help your business grow.

By Excedere Management Consultancy

The real value of outsourcing goes beyond saving money

When businesses consider outsourcing, the conversation often begins with cost savings. However, focusing exclusively on cost can overlook another valuable opportunity: access to experienced professionals with skills that are difficult to recruit locally.

For small and growing businesses, an unfilled position can place pressure on existing teams and delay plans. Looking beyond the local recruitment market can broaden the search for people with the right capabilities, relevant experience and availability.

Many professionals within the global outsourcing talent pool have previous experience supporting businesses in the United States, Australia, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and Europe. Relevant experience should always be verified during recruitment.

Experienced professionals who understand your business environment

Depending on their background, candidates may already be familiar with the processes, business culture and working hours of the companies they have supported. Relevant experience can include:

  • Industry software, operating systems and reporting procedures.
  • Customer expectations and professional communication.
  • Deadlines, quality standards and performance expectations.
  • Collaboration across time zones and agreed operating hours.

For example, a bookkeeper with previous Australian client experience may have used Xero or MYOB and worked with Australian business terminology. A customer service professional with US experience may understand the communication style and hours that a US support role requires. These are illustrative examples, rather than promises about any individual candidate.

Recruitment should establish what a person actually did, which systems they used and how closely that experience matches your role. Relevant qualifications, references and practical assessments can help inform the decision. Company specific onboarding and supervision still matter.

Closing the skills gap beyond your local market

The aim is to identify the capability your business needs, then find a person who can meet those requirements. That could mean an experienced bookkeeper, software developer, administrative professional or customer support specialist.

A broader search can create opportunities to fill specialist positions, add operational capacity and arrange coverage across time zones. Previous experience may reduce the need to teach fundamental job skills, although each person will still need to learn your systems, procedures and expectations.

Start with a clear role description. Define the responsibilities, essential skills, required qualifications, software knowledge and working hours before comparing candidates.

Why some experienced professionals choose to work from home

There is also a human side to global talent. Some experienced professionals who previously relocated to major cities for career opportunities now prefer to work remotely from their hometowns, allowing them to remain close to family while continuing their professional careers.

In the Philippines, for example, this can mean returning to a smaller town or province after developing a career in a major business centre. For some professionals, remote work offers a way to use that experience while remaining close to family and friends.

This is one possible career path, rather than a description of every remote worker. Location alone tells an employer little about capability. The relevant questions are whether the candidate has the right experience, can meet the role's requirements and has a suitable working arrangement.

Professional home offices and suitable technology

Experienced remote professionals may already have dedicated workspaces, modern computers and reliable internet connections. Employers should assess equipment, connectivity, security and backup arrangements before onboarding.

Checks should reflect the actual work. Consider computer specifications, the software required, call quality, internet performance during the intended working hours, workspace privacy and access controls. Confirm how sensitive information will be handled and what happens if power or connectivity fails.

A candidate's description of their setup is a starting point. Verify it individually and agree who provides, maintains and supports any necessary equipment. A professional home office should be assessed against the same practical expectations as any other working environment.

Office based or working from home: choose what fits

Some professionals prefer an office environment, while others prefer working from home. Businesses also have different needs. An office arrangement may suit roles that need centrally managed infrastructure, direct supervision or close team collaboration. Working from home may suit roles with clear deliverables and appropriate remote access.

The choice should reflect the responsibilities, security requirements, operating hours and preferences of the people involved. Confirm the availability and suitability of each arrangement rather than assuming one model fits every role.

At Excedere, we believe businesses should be able to explore both office and home working options as part of assessing a suitable outsourcing approach.

Cost savings remain part of the decision

Depending on the role, location and employment arrangements, outsourcing can provide opportunities to reduce overall employment costs while accessing experienced professionals.

Compare the full cost of each option, including recruitment, provider fees, equipment, management and onboarding where applicable. Savings are not guaranteed, and a lower headline salary does not establish the overall value of an arrangement.

The commercial objective is a capable professional who meets your standards within a sustainable budget. Skills, service quality and continuity deserve consideration alongside cost.

The human side of global talent

Behind each position is a person building a career and supporting their own goals. For some, international work creates professional opportunities. For others, remote work makes it possible to continue a career closer to home.

Clear expectations, fair working arrangements, appropriate support and opportunities to develop help create a sound basis for the relationship. Thinking about outsourcing as a longer term talent decision encourages businesses to consider both operational needs and the people doing the work.

Building your business beyond borders

Successful outsourcing begins with understanding what your business needs. Identify the skills gap, relevant industry experience, preferred location, working arrangement and hours the role requires. Then assess candidates and delivery options against those requirements.
